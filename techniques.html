Double Sudoku techniques

A guide to every technique used by the step-by-step hint solver. Each row, column and outlined region must contain two copies of each digit, 1–4.

Start by counting remaining copies

A unit means a row, column or outlined region. A candidate is a digit still possible in an empty cell. Remove a digit from a cell when its row, column or region already contains two copies of that digit, or when a sound earlier deduction excludes it.

For each unit, calculate remaining copies = 2 − copies already placed. A single placed 1 does not rule out another 1 in the same unit.

In the examples, {1,2} means a cell can hold either 1 or 2. Examples describe candidate patterns within one unit, rather than complete puzzles. Coordinates such as r3c5 mean row 3, column 5.

Place a digit

1. Naked single

If a cell has just one candidate, place that digit.

ExampleA cell’s row already has two 1s, its column has two 2s, and its region has two 4s. Only 3 remains, so place 3.

2. Hidden single

If a unit needs one more copy of a digit and only one empty cell can hold it, that cell must contain the digit. The cell may still have other candidates.

ExampleA row already has one 2. Only r3c5 can hold the second 2. Place 2 in r3c5.

3. Two forced copies

If a unit needs both copies of a digit and exactly two cells can hold it, fill both cells with that digit.

ExampleA region has no 4s. Its only candidates for 4 are r1c1 and r2c1. Both cells must be 4—even though they share a column, which allows two 4s.

If two copies are needed but only one cell can hold the digit, the board is contradictory; that is not a hidden single.

Naked subsets: reserve all remaining copies

Choose empty cells in one unit and collect their candidate digits. If the unit’s total remaining copies of those digits exactly equals the number of chosen cells, those cells use every remaining copy. Remove those digits from other cells in that unit.

The names below count cells, not distinct digits. If the same cells also share another unit, check that unit’s remaining counts separately before eliminating there.

4. Naked pair

Two cells reserve two remaining copies.

ExampleA row already contains one 1 and one 2. Two empty cells each have {1,2}. The row needs 1 × 1 + 1 × 2, so remove 1 and 2 from its other empty cells.

Common trap: if the row still needs two 1s and two 2s, two {1,2} cells do not form this deduction. Four copies are still needed.

5. Naked triple

Three cells reserve three remaining copies; repeated digits are allowed.

ExampleA row already has one 1 and no 2s. Three cells can contain only {1,2}. They must hold 1,2,2 in some order. Remove 1 and 2 from the other empty cells in that row.

6. Naked quadruple

Four cells reserve four remaining copies.

ExampleA region has no 1s or 2s. Four of its cells can contain only {1,2}. They must hold 1,1,2,2 in some order. No other cell in that region can be 1 or 2.

Hidden subsets: reserve the only available cells

Choose digits still needed in a unit. Find every empty cell that can hold any of those digits. If those cells exactly match the total number of required copies, they must hold those copies. Remove other digits from those cells.

7. Hidden pair

Two required copies have only two available cells.

ExampleA column needs one more 1 and one more 2. Only two cells can contain either digit, with candidates {1,2,3} and {1,2,4}. Remove 3 from the first and 4 from the second: they must hold 1 and 2.

8. Hidden triple

Three required copies have only three available cells.

ExampleA region needs one more 1 and two more 2s. Only three cells can hold 1 or 2, though they also have other candidates. Those cells must hold 1,2,2. Remove 3 and 4 from them wherever present.

9. Hidden quadruple

Four required copies have only four available cells.

ExampleA row needs two 1s and two 2s. Only four cells can hold either digit. Those cells must hold 1,1,2,2, so remove their 3 and 4 candidates.

The hint solver may explain the same elimination using a smaller or complementary naked subset first. A subset containing all four distinct digits has no other digit to eliminate in Double Sudoku.

Use the guide while solving

  1. Open a Daily, Archive or Play puzzle and select Get hint.
  2. Read the explanation. Blue outlines identify the pattern; dashed orange outlines mark cells losing candidates.
  3. Select Apply move to place digits or remove candidates. Eliminations can unlock a placement on the next step.
  4. Review Applied moves, or switch between solver candidates and your own notes.

Hints start with singles, then forced copies, then subsets of two through four cells. A last empty cell in a unit (“full house”) is covered by a single. Editing numbers resets hint deductions; handwritten notes are not treated as proof.

This is the complete guide to the current hint solver, not an exhaustive list of every possible logical argument. Some uniquely solvable puzzles need reasoning beyond these techniques. If no move is found, the solver says so and does not guess. The generator checks uniqueness, not whether this technique set can finish a puzzle.
