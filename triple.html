Triple Sudoku

Generating a uniquely solvable puzzle…

Choose a number, then tap a blank cell. Or focus a cell and type 1–3; use Backspace to erase. Notes are optional pencil marks. Red cells mean a number appears more than three times in a row, column or box.

Single-cell mode stops when no clue can be removed. Dual-cell mode removes pairs only and stops when no pair can be removed; a single removable clue may remain.

Choose a date to play its repeatable daily puzzle. Your progress is saved separately for every date.

Each row, column and box needs three copies of each digit. Always count how many copies are still needed.

Completed counts and naked singles

Once a unit contains three copies of a digit, remove that digit from its other cells. If a cell has just one candidate left, place it.

Hidden singles and forced copies

If a row needs two more 2s and exactly two cells can hold 2, both must be 2. The same rule works for one or three remaining copies.

Subsets based on remaining copies

Suppose a row needs two 1s, one 2 and one 3. If three empty cells can contain only 1 or 2, they use all those copies; the fourth cell must be 3. Two cells with candidates {1,2} are not automatically a naked pair when more than two copies remain.

Locked candidates

If a box needs two more 1s, all confined to one row, and that row also needs exactly two, remove 1 from the row outside that box. The remaining allowances must match.

Fish

When selected rows require as many copies of a digit as their covering columns can hold, the rows consume the columns’ entire remaining allowance. Remove that digit elsewhere in those columns.

Short contradiction chains

Assume a candidate, follow forced placements, and look for a definite contradiction: too many copies, too few available positions, or a cell with no candidate. A stalled search is not proof of impossibility.

Uses completed digit counts, singles, two or three forced copies, subsets based on remaining copies, locked candidates, fish and short contradiction chains. A puzzle may need deductions beyond these techniques; the walkthrough will say when it is stuck.
